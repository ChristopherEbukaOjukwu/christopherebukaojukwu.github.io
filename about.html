Research, background, and the questions behind my work.

Biography

I am a doctoral researcher in Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, working at the intersection of computational genetics, genomics, network biology, and the science of science.

My research uses large-scale genomic, biological, and scientific data to investigate how genetic variation contributes to complex traits and how scientific evidence informs biomedical discovery. I am particularly interested in the genetic architecture of complex diseases, the generalizability of genetic findings across populations, and the relationship between biological networks and genetic associations.

A complementary part of my work examines science itself. I study how research attention, publication patterns, and the reliability of scientific evidence influence the knowledge available to researchers. Across these areas, I am interested in developing computational approaches that help us better understand biological systems and evaluate the evidence underlying scientific decisions.
