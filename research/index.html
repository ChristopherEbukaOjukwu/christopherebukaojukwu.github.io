02 / Research

Research

Questions at the intersection of biology, genetics, and how scientific knowledge is produced.

My research spans computational and statistical genetics, network biology, and the science of science. I use large-scale data and computational methods to investigate the biological organization of complex traits and the processes through which scientific knowledge is produced and applied.

Research areas

Selected directions
01

Genetic ancestry & therapeutic development

I investigate how ancestry representation in genetic studies affects the interpretation and translational use of genetic evidence. My work examines the portability of genome-wide association findings across populations and the relationship between ancestry diversity, research activity, and drug development outcomes.

In an analysis of 790 genetically supported drug target–indication pairs, I found that broader ancestry representation did not independently predict drug launch after accounting for research volume. Related analyses identified attenuation of genetic effect estimates in non-European populations, highlighting challenges in generalizing genetic evidence across populations.

02

Network genetics & complex traits

I use biological networks to investigate the genetic architecture of complex traits and diseases. Working with genome-wide association data and protein–protein interaction networks, I examine how genes associated with complex traits are distributed across biological systems and how their network organization relates to genes underlying monogenic conditions.

Current work focuses on height and schizophrenia, integrating genetic association data from large cohorts with biological interaction networks. I am also developing reusable computational methods for network-based analyses of complex-trait genetics.

03

Scientific attention & the reliability of biomedical knowledge

I study how scientific research activity shapes the accumulation, organization, and reliability of biological knowledge. My work examines patterns of research attention across genes and proteins, the relationship between scientific publications and biological evidence, and the potential effects of retracted research on biomedical knowledge resources.

These projects combine approaches from the science of science, computational biology, and network analysis to better understand how the scientific record influences biological inference.
